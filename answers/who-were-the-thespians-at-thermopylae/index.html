Who Were the Thespians at Thermopylae?

The Thespians were citizens of Thespiae, a city in Boeotia in central Greece. At the Battle of Thermopylae in 480 BCE, approximately 700 Thespians fought and died alongside the 300 Spartans under Leonidas. Unlike the Spartans, the Thespians were not ordered to fight to the death. They chose to stay. Their commander was Demophilus, son of Diadromes, named by Herodotus in his Histories. The Thespian decision had a clear cause: their city lay directly in the Persian army's path south. If Xerxes passed Thermopylae, Thespiae would be destroyed regardless. The Thespians had also refused to submit to Persia before the battle, leaving them nothing to negotiate. Herodotus records that after Thermopylae, the Persians destroyed Thespiae entirely and forced its population to flee. The Thespians who died at Thermopylae are commemorated in a separate inscription from the famous Spartan epitaph. For centuries their sacrifice was overshadowed by the story of the 300, yet ancient sources including Herodotus and Diodorus Siculus are clear that the Thespian dead outnumbered the Spartan fallen more than two to one.
