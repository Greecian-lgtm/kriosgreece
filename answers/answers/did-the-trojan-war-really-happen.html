Did the Trojan War Really Happen?

Probably something happened—a conflict at Troy around 1200 BCE—but not the ten-year siege with Achilles and the wooden horse that Homer describes.

The oldest person to ask this question was Thucydides, the Athenian historian, writing in the 5th century BCE—about 700 years after the war supposedly occurred. And he had a specific problem with the story.

What Thucydides Actually Says

In his History of the Peloponnesian War (Book 1, sections 9-11), Thucydides accepts that a war happened at Troy. But he looks at the numbers and calls BS on the scale. Here's his logic:

The poet [Homer] exaggerated the size of the Greek force. The reason the war took ten years wasn't strategy—it was that the Greeks didn't have enough supplies to maintain a large army. They were too poor and scattered to keep such a force in the field for a decade. The war must have been real, but smaller and quicker than the tradition claims.

Thucydides is the first person in recorded history to do source criticism. He reads Homer like a historian reads a press release: looking for the true story buried under the hype.

What Most People Get Wrong

The misconception: If the Iliad describes it, it happened that way.

Where it comes from: We've treated Homer like scripture for 2,500 years. The Iliad is so vivid, so detailed, so *real* that it feels like eyewitness testimony. It's not. It's a poem
