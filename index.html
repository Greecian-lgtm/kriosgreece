Why did so many Greeks leave?

What the sources say

  • The first wave of emigration followed the economic crisis of 1893, when currant export prices collapsed. From 1890 to 1914 almost a sixth of Greece's population emigrated, mostly to the United States and Egypt.Migration Policy Institute, Greece: A History of Migration (Kasimis and Kassimi, 2004)
  • More than one million Greeks emigrated in a second wave, mainly between 1950 and 1974, most to Western Europe, the United States, Canada and Australia. Between 1974 and 1985 almost half of them returned to Greece.Migration Policy Institute, Greece: A History of Migration (Kasimis and Kassimi, 2004)

Still open

  • How many Greeks reached the United States in the first wave. Published histories give figures from about 430,000 to more than 600,000, so the film says "hundreds of thousands".KRIOS research notes

In the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, many Greeks left because work was scarce and they hoped to build a more secure future. Some crossed the Atlantic to the United States, planning to earn money and return.

In Greek, there's a word for life far from home: ξενιτιά. It's more than a destination on a map. It's the distance between the person who leaves and the people who stay.

From the film’s narration.

Where does the famous “Greek dance” come from?

What the sources say

  • Sirtaki was choreographed for Zorba the Greek (1964) by Giorgos Provias, drawing on existing Greek dances rather than surviving from antiquity.Wikipedia: Sirtaki

Interpretation, not established fact

  • The story of Anthony Quinn's injured foot shaping the dance comes from Quinn's own later account. It is widely repeated, but it is his recollection.Wikipedia: Sirtaki

Greece already had more dances than Hollywood could ever fit into one film. Greek dance isn't decoration sitting on top of a Greek culture. It's one of the places culture was stored.

Something invented can become tradition. Something old can change. And something genuinely ancient can disappear completely if nobody teaches it. Age doesn't guarantee survival. People do.

From the film’s narration.
