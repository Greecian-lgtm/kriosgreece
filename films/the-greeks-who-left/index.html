The Greeks Who Left, and the Greece They Carried

A suitcase can carry clothes and photographs. How do you pack a language, a song, or a church bell?

Have you ever heard someone in your family mention a village in Greece, or an island, and realized you never asked what life there was really like?

Maybe you were young. Maybe you thought there would always be time.

And then one day, there wasn't.

The story

In the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, many Greeks left because work was scarce and they hoped to build a more secure future. Some crossed the Atlantic to the United States, planning to earn money and return.

In Greek, there's a word for life far from home: ξενιτιά. It's more than a destination on a map. It's the distance between the person who leaves and the people who stay.

The Greece people remembered didn't stay frozen exactly as they'd left it. It changed with their new lives.

The people who left Greece didn't carry an entire country with them in a suitcase. They carried pieces of a life, and built something new from them.

Quoted from the film's approved narration.

What the sources tell us

Established

  • The first wave of emigration followed the economic crisis of 1893, when currant export prices collapsed. From 1890 to 1914 almost a sixth of Greece's population emigrated, mostly to the United States and Egypt.

    Migration Policy Institute, Greece: A History of Migration (Kasimis and Kassimi, 2004)

  • More than one million Greeks emigrated in a second wave, mainly between 1950 and 1974, most to Western Europe, the United States, Canada and Australia. Between 1974 and 1985 almost half of them returned to Greece.

    Migration Policy Institute, Greece: A History of Migration (Kasimis and Kassimi, 2004)

Still open

  • How many Greeks reached the United States in the first wave. Published histories give figures from about 430,000 to more than 600,000, so the film says "hundreds of thousands".
