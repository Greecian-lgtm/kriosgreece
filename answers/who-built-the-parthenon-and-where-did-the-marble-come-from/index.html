Who Built the Parthenon and Where Did the Marble Come From?

The Parthenon was built between 447 and 432 BCE under the direction of the Athenian statesman Pericles. The architects were Ictinus and Callicrates, while the sculptor Pheidias oversaw all decorative work and created the colossal chryselephantine statue of Athena housed inside. The entire structure was constructed from Pentelic marble, quarried from Mount Pentelicus approximately 16 kilometers northeast of Athens. Pentelic marble is distinguished by its fine grain and iron content, which gives the stone a warm golden tone that deepens over centuries of oxidation. Ancient sources record that over 22,000 tons of marble were quarried and transported to the Acropolis on wooden sledges along a specially constructed road. The Parthenon contains no perfectly straight lines: every horizontal surface has a slight upward curve designed to counteract the optical illusion that makes truly straight structures appear to sag. This refinement, called entasis, required every column drum and floor block to be cut to a slightly different specification. The temple was completed in 432 BCE and dedicated to Athena Parthenos.
