Were the Titans Evil?

No—Hesiod never calls them evil. They were powerful, primal, and eventually overthrown, but the moral judgment came later.

What Hesiod Actually Says

In the Theogony (circa 700 BCE), our oldest surviving account, Hesiod presents the Titans as the second generation of divine rulers. They had strengths and weaknesses, sure, but he doesn't paint them as moral villains. Cronos ruled justly during the Golden Age. Oceanus and Tethys were respected water deities. Themis represented divine law. These aren't demons—they're gods.

Here's what Hesiod says about Cronos's reign: the humans lived without suffering, without hard work, and the earth produced fruit without being tilled. That's the Golden Age under Titan rule. Not exactly an evil regime.

Where the "Evil Titans" Idea Comes From

Later writers—especially Ovid and Roman sources—reframed the Titans as primitive, violent, and destructive. The "evil Titans vs. good Olympians" binary makes for a better story arc. Marvel and modern retellings lean hard into this version because conflict sells.

But Hesiod? He's neutral. The Titans lost. That's not the same as evil.

What Most People Get Wrong

People assume Greek mythology has a clear moral universe with good gods and bad gods. It doesn't. The Olympians weren't virtuous—they were just stronger and had better PR. Zeus won the Titanomachy through military advantage, not because he was morally superior. Hesiod's Theogony is a genealogy and power struggle, not a moral epic.

The confusion usually comes from later Roman poetry and modern fantasy adaptations that imposed a good-vs-evil framework onto Greek sources that never had one.

Primary Sources

  • Hesiod, Theogony (c. 700 BCE) — Lines 113-210 on the Titans' birth; lines 590-612 on Cronos's Golden Age rule
  • Ovid, Metamorphoses (8 CE) — Book 1: where the Titans get reframed as destructive and primitive (this is where the "evil" narrative enters)

The Bottom Line

The Titans were the previous power. They ruled. They fell. Hesiod doesn't judge them. We do.
