Who built the Lion Terrace on Delos?

The Naxians built the Lion Terrace on Delos in the early 7th century BCE, carving the famous row of marble lions from Naxian stone as a dedication to Apollo. We know this because they left their name on it.

What the primary sources say

The oldest evidence comes from the marble lions themselves—and specifically from inscriptions left by later visitors. Pausanias, writing in the 2nd century CE, records that the lions were a Naxian dedication. But here's where it gets interesting: the lions themselves originally bore an inscription identifying the Naxians as donors. That inscription is fragmentary now, but it's there in the archaeological record.

The architectural style matches Naxian marble work from the same period. These aren't guesses. The stone came from Naxos. The sculptural technique matches what we see in other Naxian dedications across the Greek world. The marble quarries on Naxos were already ancient and heavily worked by this time—as we discuss in our Cyclades episode, Naxian marble was being extracted on industrial scales centuries before classical Athens existed.

What most people get wrong

The confusion usually comes from mixing up the Lion Terrace with other Delos sculptures. People read about later Hellenistic restorations and assume those represent the original builders. Or they see that Delos itself was sacred to multiple poleis and assume the lions were communal work.

They weren't. This was a Naxian statement of power and piety. When a single island-state could afford to send massive marble lions to the sanctuary of another island's god, that tells you everything about Naxian wealth and influence in the 7th century. The Naxians were mining and exporting marble at scales that made them economically dominant in the Cyclades.

Primary sources

  • Pausanias, Description of Greece 9.23.3 — Records the Naxian dedication of the lions at Delos. Pausanias visited Delos himself and saw the inscriptions.
  • Archaeological evidence: Inscribed marble fragments from Delos excavations — The base inscriptions identifying the Naxians as donors. See Delos excavation reports (École Française d'Athènes).
  • Stylistic analysis of Naxian marble work — The lions' proportions and technique match 7th-century Naxian sculpture found elsewhere.

The real story

What's remarkable isn't just that the Naxians built them. It's that they could. In the 7th century, before Athens was anything, Naxos was exporting marble across the Mediterranean and dedicating monuments to other islands' gods. That's economic and political reach most city-states never had.
