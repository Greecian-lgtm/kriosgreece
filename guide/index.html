About KRIOS

  • What is KRIOS?

    Reported from KRIOS's published page

    KRIOS makes short documentaries on Greek history, faith and family memory, for Greeks in Greece and everywhere else. It describes itself as the living story of Greek civilization, with five aims: preserve, teach, understand, connect, pass forward.

  • How does KRIOS decide what goes into a film?

    Reported from KRIOS's published page

    KRIOS says that if a claim cannot be traced to a source, it does not go in the film, and when the evidence runs out, the film says so. It prefers the oldest source to the most famous one, using primary sources and named scholarship for historical claims, checked before recording and again on the finished film.

  • How does KRIOS keep history, tradition and belief apart?

    Reported from KRIOS's published page

    • HISTORY
    • TRADITION
    • BELIEF
    • PRACTICE

    KRIOS says it separates what is documented, what the Church teaches, what families practice, and where communities differ. In this Guide, answers about contested or inherited subjects carry the labels HISTORY, TRADITION, BELIEF or PRACTICE so you can see which kind of statement you are reading.

  • When is the feast of Saint Demetrios, the name day of Dimitris and Dimitra?

    Reported from KRIOS's published page

    • PRACTICE

    KRIOS's published page gives October 26 as the feast of Saint Demetrios on the calendar followed by the Church of Greece, celebrated that day by people named Dimitris, Dimitrios and Dimitra. It adds that churches on the Old Calendar celebrate 13 days later, on November 8 of the civil calendar. The page cites one source, Saint John's Monastery.

Answers from the films

  • Why did Greeks leave Greece between 1890 and 1914?

    Answered from a named source

    • HISTORY

    The Migration Policy Institute says the first wave of emigration was spurred by the economic crisis of 1893, after a rapid fall in the price of currants. From 1890 to 1914 almost a sixth of the population of Greece emigrated, mostly to the United States and Egypt.

    Read on KRIOS

    • Sources for The Greeks Who Left

    Source

    • Migration Policy Institute: Greece, A History of Migration (Kasimis and Kassimi, 2004) (opens the source in a new tab)
  • What was the second wave of Greek emigration?

    Answered from a named source

    • HISTORY

    According to the Migration Policy Institute, more than one million Greeks emigrated in a second wave, mainly between 1950 and 1974, to Western Europe, the United States, Canada and Australia. It also says almost half of the post-war emigrants had returned to Greece.

  • What does ξενιτιά (xenitia) mean?

    Reported from KRIOS's published page

    The film and its Q&A page describe it as the Greek word for life far from home, and as the distance between the person who leaves and the people who stay. Neither page cites a dictionary or scholarly source for this, so treat it as KRIOS's description.

  • Is there a Greek dance group in the Chicago area?

    Answered from a named source

    • PRACTICE

    The Greek dance film names the Orpheus Hellenic Folklore Society. Its About page says its repertoire includes dances from every region of Greece, with an adult group of young adults from the Chicago area and a youth group for children aged 9 to 15, and that it shares them through performances, workshops and lectures.
