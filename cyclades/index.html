Small things families keep without noticing: a word, a cup of coffee, a board game, a lamp before an icon.

Words we keep

Everyday Greek words you may have heard at a family table.

  • κέφιkéfihigh spiritsThe mood that makes a table, a song or an afternoon come alive.
  • φιλοξενίαfiloxeníahospitalityMade from φίλος, friend, and ξένος, stranger or guest.
  • παρέαparéacompanyThe people you spend your time with, and the time itself.
  • κέρασμαkérasmaa treat offeredWhat a host puts in front of you before you have asked for anything.
  • καφενείοkafeneíothe coffeehouseA place for coffee, talk, cards and tavli.
  • κομπολόιkompolóiworry beadsStrung beads kept moving through the fingers, for the hands more than for counting.
  • καντήλιkandílithe small oil lampKept lit in front of the icons at home.
  • γιορτήgiortífeast, celebrationAlso what many families call a name day.

What was on the table

Cheese is part of the Cycladic table. One of them, kopanisti, has a protected designation of origin from the Cyclades. It is salty, peppery and creamy once it has ripened for a number of weeks, and it can be made from cow, sheep or goat milk, or a mix.

Your family may have a different dish that says "home" more than any other. That one matters more to this page than any list.

Source: Kopanisti, protected designation of origin cheese of the Cyclades

Coffee, unhurried

Greek coffee is made in a small long-handled pot, a μπρίκι (bríki), and served in a small cup with a layer of foam on top. You order it by how much sugar you want, and then you stay a while.

  • σκέτοςskétosno sugar
  • μέτριοςmétriosmedium sweet
  • γλυκόςglykóssweet

Method as described in common recipe and culture guides.

Tavli

Tavli is a board game for two, played with dice. It is really three games played one after another until someone reaches the agreed score.

  1. ΠόρτεςPórtesThe one closest to backgammon. You can hit a lone checker off the board.
  2. ΠλακωτόPlakotóNo hitting. You pin an opposing checker by covering it with one of your own.
  3. ΦεύγαFévgaA running game, also called Έβγα (Évga). No hitting and no pinning, both sides move the same way round the board.

Source: Tavli, the three games and how they are played

Faith at home

In many Greek Orthodox homes there is a corner for the icons, an εικονοστάσι (eikonostási), often with a small καντήλι lit before it. Name days, the feast of the saint you are named for, are marked with a call, a visit or a sweet.

Customs differ from family to family, and none of them is more Greek than another. For feast days and name days, see Sacred Days.

Ask someone in your family

Write the answers down while someone still remembers them. If nobody knows, leave the gap. Do not fill it in.

  • Who made the coffee in our family, and how did they take it?
  • Did anyone play tavli, and who did you have to beat first?
  • Is there a lamp, an icon or a small shelf at home where someone used to stop and cross themselves?
  • Which word did our family say that nobody else understood?
  • What was on the table when the whole family came together?
