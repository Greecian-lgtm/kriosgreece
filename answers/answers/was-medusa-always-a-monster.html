Was Medusa Always a Monster?

No. Medusa was human before she was a monster. Apollodorus, writing in the Bibliotheca (1st-2nd century CE), gives us the oldest surviving account of her transformation: she was a priestess in Athena's temple when Poseidon assaulted her. Athena's response was to transform Medusa into a creature with snakes for hair and a gaze that turned men to stone—not to punish Poseidon, but to punish her.

What the Primary Source Actually Says

Apollodorus tells it plainly: Poseidon violated Medusa in Athena's sanctuary. Athena, enraged, metamorphosed her victim into a monster. That's the transaction. The god gets away with assault. The woman gets turned into something people hunt and kill.

This matters because it reframes the entire myth. Medusa isn't evil. She's not born dangerous. She becomes dangerous after being wronged—first by a god, then by a goddess. By the time Perseus shows up to cut off her head as a trophy, she's already been victimized twice.

What Most People Get Wrong

The common misconception is that Medusa was always a monster, always evil, always something to be slain. This comes from later retellings and Roman sources (especially Ovid), where she's sometimes presented as simply monstrous from the start. But the Greek sources are clearer: transformation, not origin. She was made into this. It was done to her.

We read her as the villain in her own story because we learned the story from the hero's perspective—Perseus the monster-slayer. But if you go back to what Apollodorus actually wrote, the question becomes darker: why is the assault victim turned into the monster?
