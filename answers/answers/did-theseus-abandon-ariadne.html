Did Theseus abandon Ariadne?

Yes. Theseus left Ariadne sleeping on the island of Naxos after she helped him kill the Minotaur and escape Crete. But whether it was callous abandonment or divine intervention depends on which ancient source you're reading.

What the oldest sources say

Plutarch, writing around 100 CE in his Life of Theseus, gives us the account most people know: Theseus sailed away from Naxos while Ariadne slept. No explanation. No goodbye. Just gone.

But here's the thing — Plutarch wasn't the original source. He was drawing on earlier writers. The problem is that the really early versions (Bacchylides, around 500 BCE, and Euripides writing after him) sometimes claim Dionysus appeared to Theseus in a dream and told him to leave her. Or that a god instructed him to go. The divine excuse gets added or dropped depending on who's telling the story and where they're telling it.

In Athens, where Theseus was basically their founding hero, the story softened into "he forgot" or "a god made him." In other Greek cities? They just said he dumped her. No excuses.

What most people get wrong

We assume there's one "true" version of this myth. There isn't. The earliest written sources don't all agree on whether Theseus was a jerk or just divinely compelled. And that's actually the more honest answer: ancient Greeks told this story different ways depending on what point they were making.

Also — and this matters — Ariadne didn't stay abandoned. Dionysus found her on Naxos, married her, and made her immortal. So in the original myth, she gets a better ending than staying with a guy who left her unconscious on an island.

Primary sources

  • Plutarch, Life of Theseus (1st century CE) — The most detailed account. Describes Ariadne left on Naxos; attributes it variously to forgetfulness or divine will depending on the section.
  • Bacchylides, Fragment 17 (circa 500 BCE) — Claims Dionysus appeared to Theseus in a dream, ordering him to abandon Ariadne.
  • Euripides, The Suppliants (circa 420 BCE) — References Theseus leaving Ariadne, frame depends on manuscript tradition.
  • Apollodorus, Library (circa 180 BCE) — Compiles multiple versions; notes the divine dream explanation alongside the abandonment narrative.

The real story

Theseus abandoned Ariadne. The question is whether the ancient storytellers thought he should feel guilty about it. And that answer changed depending on who was doing the telling.
