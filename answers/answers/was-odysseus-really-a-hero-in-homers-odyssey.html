Was Odysseus Really a Hero in Homer's Odyssey?

What Homer Actually Shows Us

Homer's Odyssey presents Odysseus in ways that ancient readers understood quite differently than modern audiences assume. The primary text itself—particularly Books 21-22 where Odysseus slaughters the suitors—reveals a character far more complex and troubling than the "heroic wanderer" label suggests. Homer describes Odysseus grinning over corpses "like a lion over its prey," using the word gethosyne, which means savage joy rather than righteous satisfaction.

The Ancient Debate

This wasn't a hidden interpretation waiting for modern scholars. Ancient readers saw it immediately. Virgil portrays Odysseus as essentially a war criminal in the Aeneid. Cicero, in De Officiis, explicitly calls him a liar and a coward. Roman thinkers were already questioning whether Homer intended his protagonist to be genuinely heroic or deliberately showed us something darker.

The Suitor Massacre

The final books reveal Odysseus at his most violent. He doesn't simply execute the suitors—he bathes in their blood and seems to relish it. More telling: Penelope doesn't recognize her own husband after twenty years. She tests him with the olive tree bed, but this moment isn't romantic reunion. Homer shows us her genuine fear of this blood-soaked stranger. She's witnessed what he's capable of and questions whether this is actually the man she married.

The Question of Character

Homer never suggests Odysseus changed during his travels. The polytropos quality—often translated as "resourceful" or "cunning"—existed before Troy. His cleverness saved his men, yes. But it also got them all killed through poor decisions at Cyclops' island and elsewhere. The resourcefulness and the recklessness come from the same character.

Conclusion

Was Odysseus a hero? Homer's text suggests something more complicated: a man whose intelligence and determination are inseparable from his capacity for violence and cruelty. The ancients understood this. Whether that makes him heroic depends on what you think heroes are supposed to be.
