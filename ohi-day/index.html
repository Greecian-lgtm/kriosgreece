Ohi Day, October 28

On October 28, Greeks remember the morning in 1940 when Greece refused Italy's ultimatum and went to war. It is a national holiday in Greece and in Cyprus.

What happened on October 28, 1940

Just before three in the morning, the Italian ambassador, Emanuele Grazzi, arrived at the house of Prime Minister Ioannis Metaxas in Kifissia, near Athens. He carried an ultimatum from Mussolini: let Italian troops into Greece to occupy strategic points.

Metaxas did not answer with a single word. Speaking French, he told the ambassador that this meant war. The Greek people turned that refusal into one word: ΟΧΙ, “no”.

At half past five, before the deadline had even expired, Italian troops crossed from Albania into Epirus.

The refusal came from Metaxas, who had ruled Greece as a dictator since 1936. The resistance that followed belonged to the whole nation. In the mountains of the Pindus, where mules could not climb, women carried shells and supplies on their own backs.

How the day is remembered

Greeks marked the anniversary even under occupation, and after liberation it became a national holiday.

In Thessaloniki, soldiers march before the President of the Republic. In towns and villages across Greece, schoolchildren parade under blue-and-white flags.

On the same day the Church of Greece celebrates the Protection of the Theotokos (Αγία Σκέπη), a feast it placed on October 28 in 1952. It is a church feast, separate from the national commemoration.

Abroad, Greek communities gather in church and at war memorials. In Australia, doxologies and wreath-layings are held in Melbourne, Sydney and Adelaide.
