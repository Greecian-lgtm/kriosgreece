How Many Cities Are Buried at Troy?

Archaeological excavations at Hisarlik in northwestern Turkey have revealed nine distinct settlement layers, labeled Troy I through Troy IX, spanning from approximately 3000 BCE to 500 CE. Each layer represents a different city built on the ruins of the last. The layer most scholars associate with the Trojan War is Troy VIIa, dated to around 1190 BCE, which shows evidence of destruction by fire and conflict. Heinrich Schliemann, who began excavating in 1871, initially believed Troy II was Homer's city based on the gold treasure he found. Later scholarship corrected this: Troy II dates to 2400 BCE, over a thousand years before the Iliad's period. Troy VI (1700-1300 BCE) was the most impressive settlement, featuring massive limestone walls, but evidence points to earthquake destruction rather than siege. Troy VIIa, rebuilt on those foundations, remains the strongest candidate for the city Agamemnon's forces besieged. The site continued to be inhabited through the Roman period as Ilium, and Constantine briefly considered it as his new imperial capital before choosing Byzantium.
