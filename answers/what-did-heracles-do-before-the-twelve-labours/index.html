What Did Heracles Do Before the Twelve Labours?

Before the Twelve Labours, Heracles demonstrated his extraordinary nature from infancy. In the most common version of the myth, Hera sent two serpents to kill him in his crib; the infant Heracles strangled both with his bare hands. As a youth he was educated by several tutors: Linus taught him music, Castor taught weapons, and the centaur Chiron taught wisdom and archery, though some sources assign archery to Eurytus of Oechalia. According to Apollodorus' Library, Heracles killed his music teacher Linus in a rage after being struck with a lyre, an early sign of the violent temper that would define his tragedy. His first major exploit before the labours was killing the Cithaeronian Lion which had been ravaging the flocks of King Thespius of Thespiae. During a fifty-day stay with Thespius while hunting the lion, Heracles fathered fifty sons on the king's fifty daughters. He then joined the Theban army, helped defeat the Minyans of Orchomenos who had been extracting tribute from Thebes, and was rewarded with the hand of King Creon's daughter Megara in marriage. It is after this domestic period that Hera strikes him with the madness leading to the Twelve Labours.
