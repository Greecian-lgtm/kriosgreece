What Did Medusa Look Like Before She Was Cursed?

The oldest surviving source, Hesiod's Theogony (circa 700 BCE), describes Medusa as one of three Gorgon sisters but does not describe her as beautiful before any curse. The transformation narrative comes primarily from Ovid's Metamorphoses (8 CE), a Roman work written roughly seven centuries after Hesiod. In Ovid's account, Medusa was a beautiful priestess of Athena whose hair was her most celebrated feature. After Poseidon violated her in Athena's temple, Athena transformed her hair into serpents. The earlier Greek tradition in Pindar's Twelfth Pythian Ode (490 BCE) describes the Gorgons in the context of Athena inventing the aulos flute, but gives no origin story for their appearance. In archaic Greek art, all three Gorgons are depicted as monstrous from the beginning: winged, fanged, with serpents already present. The sympathetic reading of Medusa as a cursed victim is largely a Roman literary invention. Hesiod does note that unlike her immortal sisters Stheno and Euryale, Medusa alone was mortal, which is why Perseus could kill her.
