What does kleos mean in Greek?

Kleos (κλέος) means imperishable glory or undying fame—the kind of renown that outlives you because other people keep telling your story. It's not just being famous in your lifetime. It's a specific form of immortality where your name and deeds survive because culture itself carries them forward.

The Oldest Source

Homer's Iliad Book 9, line 413 is our earliest written evidence. Achilles is explaining why he won't give up his pursuit of glory, saying that even death won't stop him from achieving kleos—his deeds will live on. The exact phrase: his mother told him he had two paths, and if he chooses the one that leads to Troy, he'll gain "imperishable kleos" (κλέος ἄφθιτον). Death ends his life, but not his name.

That's the deal. You die, but if you've done something worth remembering, you become immortal through other people's voices. Your story doesn't end—it just changes form.

What Most People Get Wrong

Here's the trap: people treat kleos like it just means "fame" or "reputation." But that's backwards. Fame is what happens while you're alive. Kleos specifically means posthumous glory—the glory that matters after you're gone. You could be famous your whole life and still fail to achieve kleos if nobody remembers you after you die.

This confusion comes from modern translations that use "glory" or "honor" without the time component. Those words work fine in English until you realize the Greeks were obsessed with how long the memory lasts. Not decades. Centuries. Forever, if you're lucky.

Why It Mattered

Kleos was the actual payment for the risk. Warriors weren't fighting for land or gold—those things end with you. They were fighting to become the kind of person whose name people say out loud 300 years later. It's why Achilles chooses a short, glorious life over a long, forgotten one. That math only makes sense if you understand that kleos is a currency that doesn't devalue with time.

Primary Sources

  • Homer, Iliad 9.410–416 — Achilles on the two possible destinies and imperishable kleos
  • Homer, Iliad 2.325 — Nestor speaking about fame that endures
  • Hesiod, Theog
