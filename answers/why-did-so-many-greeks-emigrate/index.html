Why did so many Greeks emigrate?

There isn't one answer. In the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, many Greeks left because work was scarce. The first wave followed the economic crisis of 1893, when currant export prices collapsed. Most went to the United States and Egypt, and many planned to earn money and return.

A second wave, mainly between 1950 and 1974, followed the hardship of the Second World War and the Greek Civil War. More than a million Greeks left, most to Western Europe, the United States, Canada and Australia. Almost half of them had returned to Greece by 1985.

Sources

  • Migration Policy Institute, Greece: A History of Migration (Kasimis and Kassimi, 2004)
