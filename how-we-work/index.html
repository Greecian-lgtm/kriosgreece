How we work

Built on evidence, told by people

If a claim can't be traced to a source, it doesn't go in the film. When the evidence runs out, we say so.

  • The oldest source, not the most famousPrimary sources and named scholarship for every historical claim, checked before recording and again on the finished film.
  • Real archives firstHistorical photographs, documents, icons and maps, credited with their licence. Low-resolution archives are shown as framed prints, never stretched or AI-enhanced.
  • Illustration is labelledWhere no photograph exists, some everyday scenes are illustrated. They are marked on screen and never stand in for sacred or historical evidence.
  • History, tradition and belief kept apartWe say which is which: what is documented, what the Church teaches, what families practice, and where communities differ.
  • A human voiceEvery documentary today is narrated in a human voice.
  • English and GreekNew films get English and Greek captions reviewed by people, so the story reaches every generation of the family.
